# World

Continuity brief for the fiction of the game — the world, its tone, its
moral weather. Read before writing any copy, generating any art, or
making creative/narrative decisions. For sonic voice, see
`sound_design.md`.

## Overview

*The creative anchor for all copy and generative work. If a creative
decision isn't specifically contradicted here, default to this.*

### What this world is

The Realm is a shared reality that the 12 archetypes slip into. It is
not the physical world. The waking world exists but is never shown
on-screen; the game opens and closes inside the Realm.

Archetypes call the Realm **"the Trip"** in casual speech. "The Realm"
is the formal term; "the Trip" is the primary colloquial; "the Color"
is a secondary colloquial. All three are used.

It looks like a utopia. Technicolor, exaggerated, hippie-dreamscape.
Flowers the size of houses. Sunsets that last. Strangers who smile too
long. It feels unsettling beneath. Something is off, and you are in it.

Every run is a pass through the Realm. The Realm reshuffles between
runs — fresh arrangement, same Realm.

### Setting

- **Place:** The Realm (colloquially, "the Trip" or "the Color"). Not
  Earth, not a planet. A shared dreamscape that archetypes slip into
  from an unseen waking life.
- **Spatial structure:** The Realm contains roughly **30 Regions**,
  drawn from **6–8 Region types** (archetypes that share visual /
  sonic / encounter palette). A single run visits **7 Regions** along
  an FTL-style node path. "The Realm" and "Region" are the only
  spatial nouns — no tier above the Realm, no tier between Realm and
  Region.
- **Era / time:** Non-literal. Aesthetic touchstones: late-1960s
  hippie utopia exaggerated — Woodstock × Yellow Submarine × *The
  Wizard of Oz* first-color moment × Psychonauts 2's PSI King's
  Sensorium × acid trip. Logic bends.
- **Scale:** Unbounded and slippery. Geography is experiential, not
  cartographic.
- **Mood register:** Sincerely blissful on the surface. Quietly wrong
  underneath. Not dystopian. Not grimdark. Not ironic. The technicolor
  is real; the unease is also real; both are true at once.

### Central premise

- Archetypes wake up inside the Realm. **They have no memory of who
  they were.** They feel they came from somewhere but cannot access
  it. They are someone new.
- The core dramatic engine is **self-discovery**: figuring out who
  you are in this place, what this place is, how to move through it.
- Combat is **personal.** Each archetype is haunted by their own
  Anomalies — **spirits native to the Realm that latch onto
  individual archetypes.** The Anomalies know things the archetype
  cannot remember. External to the archetype, intimate anyway. They
  manifest in encounters and dissolve when the encounter ends.
- A run is a passage through the Realm, not a war against it.

### Slipperiness

"Slippery" is an experiential quality, not a level-design mechanic:

- The Realm is new and unrecognizable, yet somehow familiar.
- Things keep changing. Perception cannot be trusted. The archetype
  cannot fully trust themselves inside this new reality.
- This is the feel, not a literal rule that geography shuffles
  mid-run.

### How music works in the Realm

- Music is the Realm's **native language.** Everything vibrates.
  Colors are notes. Reality is composed.
- Music is also the archetypes' **craft and communion.** It is how
  they touch the Realm, and how the Realm touches them. Combat is
  performance; performance is contact.
- Music here is **exaggerated**, as everything is. Sounds bend.
  Chords have color. A held note can move a mountain.
- **Music-as-combat lineage:** *Brütal Legend* is the direct
  touchstone for music-as-a-weapon. We do not share its tone or
  world, but we share its central claim: playing is fighting.
- In combat, music is diegetic (see `sound_design.md`).

### The archetypes

- 12 playable characters. Each is an instrument, and **the instrument
  IS the archetype** — The Guitar, The Drummer, The DJ, The Harp, The
  Violin, The Cello, The Bass, The Upright Bass, The Keyboard, The
  Sax, The Accordion, The Singer.
- These are not individuals. They are universal patterns — closer to
  tarot cards than to named people. The instruments carry the
  symbolic weight; there is no hidden archetype layer beneath the
  instrument.
- Multiple instances of the same archetype can coexist. Two Harps may
  walk into the Realm in the same run; both are The Harp.

### Voice and copy texture

- Archetypes speak in **wisdom-sayings** sometimes — quiet, knowing,
  a little off. Sample: *"Time seems to lose meaning when you've
  been here as long as us."*
- **Pastiche is fine in small doses.** Hippie cadence as texture is
  OK; don't write "far out, man" parody dialogue. Wisdom-aphorism
  beats catchphrase-hippie.

### Mysteries (deliberately unresolved in-fiction)

These are **kept mysterious on purpose.** Lean into mystery rather
than inventing an answer.

- Who or what, if anything, created The Realm.
- Who or what, if anything, rules it.
- Why archetypes slip in, and whether they are called or drift in.
- Whether archetypes will ever return to their waking life.
- What the Anomalies *want*. They haunt; what they want is opaque.

### What this world is NOT

- Not a medieval fantasy kingdom. No knights, no castles, no dragons.
- Not cyberpunk or science fiction. No tech-mysticism, no neon
  cities.
- Not ironic or postmodern about its hippie aesthetic. The color,
  the love, the cosmic-ness are sincere. The unease is not a wink.
- Not a utopia in the moral sense. It looks utopian. It is not a
  safe place to be.
- Archetypes are not individuals. Do not write them as named people
  with personal backstories; their "history" is what they are
  becoming inside the Realm, not what they were before it.
- **Not a literal drug experience.** "Acid trip" and "the Trip" are
  stylistic references, not in-fiction explanations. Archetypes did
  not take anything. Do not write drug-use copy.

## Aesthetics

*Non-sonic. Sonic aesthetics live in `sound_design.md`. This section
anchors the visual and prose voice for generative work.*

### Color palette

- **Impossibly saturated.** Technicolor that feels almost wrong to
  look at. If a color in your prompt could be muted, it shouldn't be.
- **Warm-weighted.** Pastel rainbows, rose-pink, electric green,
  gold-hour orange, cornflower, coral. Sunset hours, not midday.
- **No grey-brown. No desaturation.** The Realm has no drab. When
  things get unsettling, the palette does not dim — it distorts (one
  color punches out, a hue shifts wrong).

### Shape language

- **Organic, flowing, melting.** Curves over hard angles. Geometry
  bends. 60s psychedelic poster art — Heinz Edelmann, Peter Max, Wes
  Wilson — is the direct lineage.
- **Exaggerated scale.** Flowers bigger than houses. Houses inside
  other houses. Horizons that curve the wrong way.
- **Breathing.** Nothing is fully static. At rest, things sway,
  pulse, drift at the edges of vision.

### Light

- **Always warm.** Sunset, dream-hour, gold-hour. Glow, not shadow.
- **When unease surfaces, light stays warm.** The wrongness is not
  darkness. It is a too-long shadow in a sunlit field, a face that
  doesn't blink.

### Prose voice

- **Reverent but not solemn.** The Realm is sacred-feeling, not
  church-serious.
- **Poetic with concrete anchors.** A metaphor should carry a
  sense-image ("the sun remembered us," not "transcendent light").
- **Dream-logic acceptable.** Gentle non-sequiturs and category
  errors land right.
- **Wisdom-sayings welcome** (see Overview → Voice and copy texture).
  Pastiche sparingly.

### What to avoid visually

- Photorealism. The Realm is painted, not rendered.
- Gritty textures, dirt, rust, noise. The Realm is clean-feeling
  even when it's wrong.
- Muted or desaturated palettes.
- Harsh geometry, hard shadows, tactical-shooter palettes.
- Cyberpunk neon-glow (different from psychedelic color).
- Medieval fantasy silhouettes.

### Visual references

Grouped by what each artist contributes to a generative image prompt.

**Late-60s psychedelic poster canon (the visual DNA):**

- **Heinz Edelmann** (*Yellow Submarine*, 1968) — saturated color
  fields against organic flat shapes; the lineage entry point.
- **Peter Max** — cosmic-pop poster art; warm-saturated rainbows,
  star-and-figure compositions.
- **Wes Wilson** — molten Fillmore typography; letters flowing into
  organic shape.
- **Victor Moscoso** — vibrating complementary colors that almost
  hurt to look at. Direct fit for *impossibly saturated*.
- **Alton Kelley** (Family Dog / Mouse Studios) — flowing nature
  compositions; warm side of the SF poster scene.
- **Hapshash and the Coloured Coat** (Nigel Waymouth + Michael
  English) — UK psych poster collective; mystical, art-nouveau-curve
  heavy.
- **Bob Masse** — Vancouver psych posters; flowing female figures in
  nature; art-nouveau-leaning warmth.

**Art-nouveau ancestors (the formal vocabulary the psych poster
artists were reviving):**

- **Alphonse Mucha** — flowing decorative panels; direct ancestor of
  Wilson and Moscoso's curvilinear linework.
- **Gustav Klimt** — gold, ornamental, sincere-mystic register;
  "reverent but not solemn" translated to image.
- **Jan Toorop** — symbolist flowing line work; hand-drawn figure
  silhouettes that feel non-human-scale.

**Surreal landscape / dreamscape ("horizons that curve the wrong way"):**

- **Moebius** (Jean Giraud) — dream-logic landscapes with clean
  line; the strongest non-poster anchor.
- **Roger Dean** — biomorphic alien environments; the
  flowers-bigger-than-houses, floating-island energy. Take the
  landscape language; leave the prog-fantasy connotation.

**Visionary / synesthetic:**

- **Keiichi Tanaami** — Japanese psychedelic pop; saturated AND
  carrying the "looks utopian, feels unsettling" undertone the Realm
  is built on.

**Non-artist anchors:**

*The Wizard of Oz* first-Technicolor reveal (the moment of color
arriving), Psychonauts 2's PSI King's Sensorium (synesthetic music-
as-landscape, closest contemporary game reference), classic
Woodstock-era concert posters as a category.

## Values

*What the archetypes are moving toward, and what keeps catching at
them. The moral and emotional grain of the Realm.*

### What drives the archetypes

- **Self-discovery.** Figuring out who they are now, in this place.
  Not recovering who they were — they have no memory of that — but
  *becoming* the archetype they inhabit.
- **Communion.** Touching and being touched by the Realm, through
  music. Contact with each other, with the Realm, with the
  Anomalies.
- **Presence.** Being here now. The Realm rewards presence;
  avoidance softens the Color around you.
- **Mastery of the instrument.** The instrument is the archetype.
  Growing into the instrument is growing into the self.

### Recurring conflicts

- **Being haunted by things that know you.** Anomalies know things
  the archetype cannot remember. Every encounter asks: how can this
  know me?
- **Perception cannot be trusted.** The Realm is slippery. What you
  just saw may not have been there. Your own feelings can feel
  borrowed.
- **Being someone new.** Waking up without a past is liberating and
  terrifying. What are you, if you are only what you are now?
- **The bliss is not safety.** The technicolor is sincere, but it
  doesn't protect you. The Realm is beautiful and dangerous in the
  same breath.

### What the Realm rewards

- Being in it, fully. Playing, listening, noticing.
- Playing together. Band-composition bonuses are the mechanical
  payoff; the fiction is that the Realm likes it when archetypes
  harmonize.
- Meeting Anomalies without fleeing. They know something; staying
  present is how it gets said.

### What the Realm punishes

- Not as a moral system — the Realm is not righteous. The punishment
  is the Realm itself when it turns on you. Fall into enough
  dissonance and the Color fades. That is the stakes structure.
